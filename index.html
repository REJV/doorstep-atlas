Where in America are households most exposed to door-to-door sales pressure?

This atlas maps a composite susceptibility index built from five publicly documented demographic correlates: share of residents 65 and older, rurality, homeownership, household isolation, and income. Pan and zoom to inspect any state, county, or neighborhood. Adjust the weights at left to see how the picture shifts when the inputs are re-prioritized.

The score is illustrative, not predictive. See methodology below.

    Methodology & sources

    How the index is built

    Inputs

    Five demographic factors are estimated per county and per state, modeled on US Census American Community Survey patterns: percent of population aged 65 and older, percent of population classified as rural, owner-occupied housing rate, percent of households with one resident, and median household income (inverted, so lower income contributes more to the score).

    Composite

    Each factor is normalized to a 0–100 scale, then combined as a weighted average using the slider weights. The default weighting (30/25/20/15/10) reflects the relative emphasis these factors receive in FTC and AARP literature on consumer fraud targeting, but every weight is user-adjustable.

    Why these factors

    Federal Trade Commission Consumer Sentinel reports, AARP fraud surveys, and academic work on door-to-door sales — particularly in solar, alarm, energy, and home-improvement canvassing — consistently identify these five demographic features as elevating exposure. None of them describe an individual's likelihood of buying. They describe community-level exposure to the channel.

    Important caveat. No federal agency publishes a county-level susceptibility ranking. The values shown here are illustrative composites built from demographic correlates, not direct observation of sales activity or fraud incidence. Treat this as a tool for thinking, not a predictive model.

    Geography
